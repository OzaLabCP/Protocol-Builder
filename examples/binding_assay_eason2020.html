gSTEP variant screen — ranking STEPtag binding by fluorescence (ΔF/F0)

Source: Eason et al., ACS Synth. Biol. 2020 — 10.1021/acssynbio.0c00407

Rank a handful (2–8) of gSTEP biosensor variants by their relative binding to STEPtag. Each variant, fixed at 75 nM, is challenged with one shared STEPtag dilution series so all variants are compared on the same axis; variants are ranked by fluorescence dynamic range (ΔF/F0) and apparent affinity. Tuned for comparison across variants, not a precise single-pair Kd.

Estimated duration: ~3 h for up to 8 variants on 1–2 plates (excludes protein expression/purification)

Procedure

  1. 1

    Prepare assay buffer

    stated

    Prepare 20 mM sodium phosphate buffer with 50 mM NaCl, pH 7.4; filter-sterilize. Used as both storage and assay diluent.

  2. 2

    Normalize and prepare each variant at 75 nM

    stated

    Quantify each purified gSTEP variant by A280 (ProtParam extinction coefficients) and dilute every variant to an identical 75 nM working concentration in assay buffer. Equal biosensor concentration across variants is what makes ΔF/F0 comparable.

    gSTEP concentration75 nM stated
    Stated; held identical across all variants.
    Number of gSTEP variants2–8 user input
    User is screening a handful of variants; sets plate layout and whether a second plate is needed.
    gSTEP working-solution volume per variant~1 mL each default verify
    Scaling per variant: 6 titrant points x 3 replicates = 18 wells; 18 x 100 uL = 1.8 mL... but a shared partner series means each variant only supplies its own 75 nM gSTEP to ~9 wells (partner added separately) -> ~1 mL + dead volume. Verify against your final layout.
    ⚠ Keep variants on ice and shielded from light until dispensed.
  3. 3

    Prepare ONE shared STEPtag master dilution series

    user input

    Because affinities are unknown and the goal is ranking, prepare a single wide STEPtag dilution series and apply it identically to every variant. Run a quick pilot on one representative variant first to center the bracket.

    3.1 Pilot: run one representative variant across the full ~3 nM–10 uM range to find where its response transitions, then keep or narrow the bracket for the full screen. default verify
    3.2 Prepare the master series once in a reservoir/dilution plate so every variant sees the identical STEPtag concentrations (multichannel transfer). best practice
    Shared STEPtag titration bracket~3 nM to 10 uM, 6-point (~4-fold steps) user input
    Wide bracket chosen because expected Kd is unknown; a sparse 6-point series is enough to rank variants. Center it after the pilot; flag to verify.
    ⚠ If any variant's ΔF/F0 is still rising at the top concentration, its binding is weaker than the bracket covers — extend the top point before ranking it.
  4. 4

    Lay out the plate(s)

    stated

    For each variant, combine 75 nM gSTEP with the shared STEPtag series across a row (or block) of the Fluotrac plate. Add one BSA control series per plate and, per variant, buffer-only and gSTEP-only (F0) wells. If variants span two plates, repeat one reference variant on both plates for normalization.

  5. 5

    Equilibrate

    best practice

    Incubate all plates under identical conditions to reach binding equilibrium before reading.

    ⏱ 30 min · 🌡 25 C
    Equilibration time and temperature30 min at 25 C best practice
    Not stated in the source. Standard-practice default; keep it identical across all variants and plates so ΔF/F0 is comparable. Verify equilibrium is reached for your tightest binders.
  6. 6

    Read fluorescence (fixed gain across the whole screen)

    stated

    Read every plate on the Tecan Infinite M1000 with excitation at 485 nm and emission at 515 nm. Set the gain once on the brightest bound well across ALL variants and hold it fixed for every plate.

    🌡 25 C
    Excitation wavelength485 nm stated
    Stated.
    Emission wavelength515 nm stated
    Stated.
    Fixed reader gain across variantsset once on the brightest well; hold constant for all plates best practice
    Comparability requirement for a screen: if gain varies between variants or plates, ΔF/F0 is no longer comparable. Verify no wells saturate at the chosen gain.
    ⚠ A different gain per variant invalidates the ranking — this is the most common screen mistake.
  7. 7

    Rank the variants

    stated

    Subtract the BSA control background, then for each variant compute ΔF/F0 = (F_saturated − F0)/F0 as the primary ranking metric. Optionally fit each sparse series to the ligand-depletion Hill equation for an apparent Kd as a secondary sort. Rank variants by ΔF/F0, then apparent affinity.

    Primary ranking metric (ΔF/F0)ΔF/F0 = (F_sat − F0)/F0 best practice
    For a variant screen, dynamic range (ΔF/F0) is the primary readout; the paper reports ΔF/F0 alongside Kd. Apparent Kd from a sparse fit is a coarse secondary sort only.
    Fitting modelHill equation accounting for ligand depletion stated
    The source specifies this fit (cites reference 35). Use total concentrations; depletion is modeled.
    ⚠ Tight binders (apparent Kd ≤ ~75 nM) are ligand-depletion-limited at 75 nM biosensor: their apparent Kd is compressed and rankings among the very tightest binders may be unreliable. Lower the biosensor concentration to separate them.

Open questions

  • Expected Kd is unknown — the pilot on one variant is required to center the shared bracket before the full screen.
  • Ligand depletion at 75 nM biosensor compresses apparent Kd for tight binders (Kd ≤ ~75 nM); to rank the tightest binders precisely, repeat them at a lower biosensor concentration.
  • Replicate count: 3 gives the paper's rigor; 2 fits more variants per plate for a first-pass rank — your call on the precision/throughput trade.
